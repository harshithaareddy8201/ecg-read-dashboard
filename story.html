What an ECG strip is trying to tell you

From one heartbeat to a hundred thousand of them: how a prototype built on our paper, A Comprehensive Survey on ECG Signal Graph Interpretation (2022), turns a raw heart recording into plain words, and what testing it on 44 patients taught us. Scroll to read, or explore the interactive dashboard.

1 · The recording

This is ten seconds of a heart

A strip from Record 100 of the public MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database. The grid is the same as on a hospital printout: each large square is 0.2 seconds across and 0.5 millivolts tall.

Most patients only ever see one word printed above a strip like this: "normal" or "abnormal".

10 · What's next

Where this goes from here

First, fix atrial-beat detection with features from the P-wave and a second ECG lead. Then test whether patients actually understand the wording, in English, Hindi and Kannada. Later, read a phone photo of a paper ECG, so no file is needed at all.

The measure of success is simple: after reading the result, does the patient know what to do next?
