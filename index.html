ECG Report Explainer · working prototype

An ECG strip, explained in plain words

Most patients get an ECG printout that only says "normal" or "abnormal". This prototype reads the heartbeat signal itself, finds every beat, flags the ones that arrive early or look unusual, and says what that means in everyday language. It builds on our paper, A Comprehensive Survey on ECG Signal Graph Interpretation (2022), and runs on real recordings from the public MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database, tested across 44 patients.

Built by Harshitha A Reddy · Code on GitHub

Try it: switch between the two recordings, flip between the patient and doctor views, read the patient view in Hindi or Kannada, pick a flagged beat on the ECG trace, and drag the sliders in "Tuning the alarm" to see the trade-off between catching problems and raising false alarms. What is an extra beat?

Sometimes the heart fires a beat a little early. Most people have a few of these and never feel them. They matter when there are many of them, or when you also feel unwell.

What to do next

No action needed. Keep up your usual check-ups.

Book a doctor's appointment in the next few days and take this report with you.

Product thinking

How I would take this from prototype to product.

Who it's for

People who receive an ECG printout but can't see a cardiologist quickly, especially outside big cities, and the general physicians who see them first.

The problem

A label like "abnormal" tells people nothing about what to do. They either panic and queue at a hospital, or ignore it.

North Star metric

Share of users who correctly say what they will do next after reading their result. Proposed target: 80% in a comprehension test.

Guardrails

  • Dangerous beats missed: aim for none
  • False-alarm rate on normal recordings
  • Time to understand the result: under 30 seconds

Key trade-off

A miss is worse than a false alarm, but too many alarms erode trust and flood clinics. The tuning section shows where the line sits today.

Roadmap

  • Now: prototype tested on 44 patients; a model that catches ventricular beats with few false alarms
  • Next: fix atrial-beat detection (P-wave features, a second ECG lead); test the wording with 10 patients in Hindi and Kannada
  • Later: read a phone photo of a paper ECG; share the summary with a doctor; clinical validation partner

How it works

  1. Clean the signal. Filter out baseline wander and electrical noise from the raw lead II trace (360 samples a second).
  2. Find each heartbeat. Detect the R-peak, the tall spike of every beat, with the NeuroKit2 peak detector.
  3. Flag early beats. A beat is early when the gap before it is under 85% of the median of the previous 8 gaps.
  4. Flag unusual shapes. Compare every beat with this person's median beat; a shape match under 0.80 counts as unusual.
  5. Train a model. A random forest learns from the timing and shape of each beat. It trained on 22 patients and was tested on 22 others it had never seen.
  6. Explain the result. Turn heart rate and the share of irregular beats into a green, amber or red message a patient can act on.

Limits: one ECG lead, a research dataset recorded decades ago, and weak atrial-beat detection, so this shows the idea rather than a clinical tool.
